Cell biology

Tier 1

Membranes and transport

  • Fluid mosaic model (Singer & Nicolson, 1972). Cholesterol buffers fluidity: it stiffens at high temperature and prevents packing at low temperature.
  • The bilayer is asymmetric: phosphatidylserine (PS) sits on the inner leaflet. PS on the outer leaflet is an early apoptosis signal, detected by Annexin V.
  • Lateral diffusion is fast; flip-flop between leaflets is slow and needs flippases or scramblases. Frye & Edidin’s mouse–human heterokaryon experiment showed lateral diffusion.
  • Simple diffusion: small nonpolar molecules (O₂, CO₂, steroids). Facilitated diffusion (GLUT carriers, channels) is saturable and needs no energy.
  • P-type ATPases phosphorylate themselves: Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, per ATP; inhibited by ouabain and digoxin), SERCA, gastric H⁺/K⁺-ATPase. V-type ATPases acidify lysosomes and vacuoles. F-type is ATP synthase. ABC transporters include MDR1 (drug efflux) and CFTR (an ABC-family chloride channel).
  • Secondary active transport uses an ion gradient: SGLT1 (2 Na⁺ : 1 glucose symport, intestine and kidney); Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger (3 Na⁺ in : 1 Ca²⁺ out, antiport). Aquaporins carry water (Agre).

Organelles and protein sorting

  • Secretory pathway: signal peptide → SRP binds and pauses translation → SRP receptor → Sec61 translocon in the rough ER. N-linked glycosylation starts in the ER on Asn in Asn-X-Ser/Thr, with the oligosaccharide pre-built on dolichol phosphate. O-linked glycosylation (Ser/Thr) happens in the Golgi.
  • Sorting signals: KDEL (soluble ER proteins retrieved from Golgi); KKXX (ER membrane proteins); nuclear localisation signal PKKKRKV (SV40 T antigen), imported through nuclear pores with importins and Ran-GTP; mitochondrial matrix proteins carry an amphipathic N-terminal presequence (TOM and TIM complexes); peroxisomal matrix proteins end in SKL (PTS1); lysosomal enzymes get mannose-6-phosphate in the cis-Golgi (missing in I-cell disease, so the enzymes are secreted).
  • Vesicle coats: COPII (ER → Golgi), COPI (Golgi → ER and between cisternae), clathrin with adaptor proteins (plasma membrane endocytosis and trans-Golgi → endosome; dynamin pinches off the vesicle). Rab GTPases target vesicles; v-SNARE and t-SNARE pairing drives fusion; NSF and SNAP pull SNAREs apart afterwards.
  • Smooth ER: lipid synthesis, cytochrome P450 detoxification, Ca²⁺ store (sarcoplasmic reticulum in muscle). Lysosome lumen pH ≈ 4.5–5 with acid hydrolases. Peroxisomes: β-oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids, catalase; Zellweger syndrome when import fails.
  • Mitochondria: double membrane; cardiolipin marks the inner membrane; human mtDNA ≈ 16.5 kb encoding 37 genes (13 proteins, 22 tRNAs, 2 rRNAs); maternal inheritance; endosymbiotic origin from α-proteobacteria. Chloroplasts: thylakoids, own circular DNA, from cyanobacteria.

Cell cycle and its control

  • G1 → S → G2 → M; G0 is quiescence. Restriction point in late G1.
  • G1/S: cyclin D–CDK4/6 starts phosphorylating Rb, cyclin E–CDK2 completes it; free E2F turns on S-phase genes. DNA damage → p53 stabilised → p21 inhibits CDK2.
  • G2/M: cyclin B–CDK1 (MPF, first found in frog oocytes). Wee1 adds inhibitory phosphates (Thr14/Tyr15); Cdc25 removes them to activate.
  • Spindle assembly checkpoint: unattached kinetochores make Mad2/BubR1 inhibit APC/C–Cdc20. Once all attach, APC/C ubiquitinates securin (separase then cleaves cohesin → anaphase) and cyclin B (exit from mitosis).
  • CDK inhibitors: INK4 family (p16) blocks CDK4/6; Cip/Kip family (p21, p27, p57) blocks broadly. Nobel 2001: Hartwell (budding yeast cdc genes), Nurse (fission yeast cdc2), Hunt (cyclins in sea urchin).
  • Meiosis I prophase: leptotene (condensation), zygotene (synapsis, synaptonemal complex), pachytene (crossing over), diplotene (chiasmata visible; oocytes arrest here), diakinesis. Meiosis I is reductional; non-disjunction gives aneuploidy.

Cell death

  • Intrinsic pathway: stress → BH3-only proteins (Bid, Bad, Puma, Noxa) → Bax/Bak pores → cytochrome c release → Apaf-1 apoptosome → caspase-9 → executioner caspases-3/7. Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL block it.
  • Extrinsic pathway: Fas ligand or TNF → death receptor → FADD → caspase-8. Caspase-8 cleaves Bid to tBid, linking to the intrinsic pathway.
  • Caspases are cysteine proteases that cut after aspartate. Apoptosis signs: shrinkage, blebbing, chromatin condensation, DNA ladder in ~180 bp steps, PS exposure, no inflammation. Necrosis: swelling, lysis, inflammation.

Signal transduction

  • GPCRs (7 transmembrane). Gs → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA; Gi inhibits adenylyl cyclase; Gq → PLCβ → IP₃ (Ca²⁺ release from ER) + DAG (activates PKC with Ca²⁺). Cholera toxin ADP-ribosylates Gsα (locked on); pertussis toxin ADP-ribosylates Giα (cannot inhibit). β-arrestin and GRKs desensitise.
  • Receptor tyrosine kinases: ligand → dimerisation → autophosphorylation → SH2 adaptor Grb2 → SOS (a GEF) → Ras-GTP → Raf → MEK → ERK. GAPs switch Ras off; oncogenic Ras has lost GTPase activity. PI3K → PIP₃ → Akt (survival); PTEN removes the 3-phosphate.
  • Other pathways: JAK–STAT (cytokines), TGF-β (receptor Ser/Thr kinases → SMADs), Wnt (β-catenin saved from the APC/Axin/GSK3 destruction complex), Notch (γ-secretase releases NICD), Hedgehog (Patched releases inhibition of Smoothened), NO → soluble guanylyl cyclase → cGMP. Steroid and thyroid hormones use intracellular nuclear receptors with zinc-finger DNA-binding domains.

Traps examiners use

  • COPII is ER → Golgi, COPI is the reverse. Students swap them.
  • Taxol stabilises microtubules; it does not depolymerise them. It still arrests mitosis.
  • Kinesin is plus-end directed; dynein minus-end. In axons, plus ends point to the terminal.
  • IP₃ releases Ca²⁺; DAG activates PKC. Neither acts on the other’s target.
  • Cholera toxin hits Gs; pertussis toxin hits Gi. Both raise cAMP.

Molecular biology and gene expression

Tier 1

DNA structure and the classic experiments

  • B-DNA: right-handed, ~10.5 bp per turn, 3.4 nm pitch, major and minor grooves. A-form: dehydrated, RNA–DNA hybrids and dsRNA. Z-form: left-handed, GC repeats.
  • Griffith (transformation, 1928) → Avery, MacLeod & McCarty (the principle is DNA, 1944) → Hershey & Chase (³²P DNA enters, ³⁵S protein stays out, 1952) → Meselson & Stahl (semiconservative, ¹⁵N/¹⁴N in a CsCl gradient, 1958). After 1 generation in ¹⁴N: all hybrid; after 2: half hybrid, half light.

  • Pol I has three activities; the Klenow fragment keeps polymerase and 3′→5′ proofreading but loses 5′→3′ exonuclease. All DNA polymerases synthesise 5′→3′ and need a primer.
  • Gyrase (bacterial type II topoisomerase) adds negative supercoils and is the quinolone target. Topo I cuts one strand, topo II both strands. Termination in E. coli at ter sites bound by Tus.
  • Telomerase: a reverse transcriptase (TERT) carrying its own RNA template; extends the 3′ G-rich overhang. Mitochondrial DNA is copied by Pol γ.
  • Repair: mismatch repair (MutS, MutL, MutH; the parental strand is identified by Dam methylation of GATC); base excision (glycosylase then AP endonuclease; uracil-DNA glycosylase); nucleotide excision for bulky lesions and thymine dimers (UvrABC; defective in xeroderma pigmentosum); photolyase reverses dimers directly; double-strand breaks by NHEJ (Ku70/80, error-prone) or homologous recombination (RecA in bacteria, Rad51, BRCA1/2).

Transcription

  • Bacterial RNA polymerase: core α₂ββ′ω plus σ⁷⁰. Promoter −10 TATAAT (Pribnow box) and −35 TTGACA. Termination: intrinsic (GC-rich hairpin followed by U-run) or Rho-dependent (Rho helicase). Rifampicin binds the β subunit; actinomycin D intercalates.
  • Eukaryotic polymerases: Pol I → 28S, 18S, 5.8S rRNA (nucleolus); Pol II → mRNA, most snRNAs, miRNAs; Pol III → tRNA, 5S rRNA, U6 snRNA, 7SL RNA. α-amanitin: Pol II very sensitive, Pol III moderately, Pol I resistant.
  • Pol II initiation: TATA box (~−25 to −30) bound by TBP within TFIID; TFIIH has helicase activity and phosphorylates CTD Ser5 at initiation; Ser2 phosphorylation marks elongation. Enhancers act at a distance through Mediator.
  • mRNA processing: 5′ cap (7-methylguanosine, 5′–5′ triphosphate); poly(A) signal AAUAAA; splicing by the spliceosome (U1 binds the 5′ site GU, U2 the branch-point A, U4/U6, U5), lariat formed by a 2′–5′ bond from the branch-point adenosine. Group I self-splicing introns use an external guanosine. RNA editing: apolipoprotein B C→U makes the shorter ApoB-48 in the gut.

Translation

  • Ribosomes: 70S = 30S (16S rRNA) + 50S (23S, 5S); 80S = 40S (18S) + 60S (28S, 5.8S, 5S). S values do not add.
  • Bacterial initiation: Shine–Dalgarno (AGGAGG) pairs with the 3′ end of 16S rRNA; initiator is fMet-tRNA. Eukaryotes: eIF4E binds the cap, the 43S complex scans to the first AUG in a Kozak context.
  • Peptidyl transferase is the 23S rRNA (a ribozyme). EF-Tu (eEF1A) delivers aminoacyl-tRNA; EF-G (eEF2) translocates; diphtheria toxin ADP-ribosylates eEF2. Release factors: RF1 (UAA, UAG), RF2 (UAA, UGA); eRF1 reads all three.
  • Cost: 4 high-energy bonds per peptide bond (2 for charging, ATP → AMP; 1 GTP for EF-Tu; 1 GTP for EF-G).
  • Code: 64 codons, 61 sense, 3 stops; degenerate; wobble at the codon’s 3rd position (anticodon’s 5′ base); inosine pairs with U, C or A.
  • Antibiotics: streptomycin (30S, misreading), tetracycline (blocks A site on 30S), chloramphenicol (50S peptidyl transferase), erythromycin (50S exit tunnel), puromycin (aminoacyl-tRNA mimic; both ribosome types), cycloheximide (80S only).

Gene regulation

  • lac operon (lacZ, lacY, lacA): LacI repressor binds the operator; allolactose or IPTG removes it. CAP–cAMP activates when glucose is low (catabolite repression). Highest expression: lactose present, glucose absent.
  • Mutants: lacI⁻ constitutive but recessive (a wild-type I⁺ on an F′ restores inducibility, since repressor is diffusible); lacOᶜ constitutive and cis-dominant (only affects genes on its own DNA); lacIˢ super-repressor, non-inducible and dominant.
  • trp operon: repressible (Trp is the corepressor) plus attenuation. Leader peptide has two Trp codons; with plenty of Trp the ribosome covers region 2 and the 3:4 terminator hairpin forms; with little Trp the ribosome stalls on region 1 and the 2:3 antiterminator forms.
  • Phage λ: cI repressor (lysogeny) versus Cro (lytic); the decision depends on cII stability.
  • Chromatin: nucleosome = 147 bp around an octamer of H2A, H2B, H3, H4 (two each); H1 binds linker DNA. Acetylation (HATs) opens chromatin, HDACs close it. H3K4me3 marks active promoters; H3K27me3 (Polycomb) and H3K9me3 (bound by HP1) are repressive. DNA methylation at CpG: DNMT1 maintains, DNMT3A/3B set new marks. Imprinting and X-inactivation (Xist RNA) rely on these.
  • Small RNAs: pri-miRNA → Drosha/DGCR8 in the nucleus → pre-miRNA → Exportin-5 → Dicer in the cytoplasm → ~22 nt duplex → Argonaute in RISC. siRNAs (perfect match) cleave mRNA; miRNAs (partial match, 3′ UTR seed) mostly repress translation. RNAi: Fire & Mello, Nobel 2006.

Mutation

  • Transitions: purine ↔ purine or pyrimidine ↔ pyrimidine. Transversions: purine ↔ pyrimidine.
  • Mutagens: 5-bromouracil (base analogue, A:T → G:C transitions), 2-aminopurine (transitions), EMS (alkylates G, G:C → A:T), nitrous acid (deaminates C → U and A → hypoxanthine), acridines and proflavin (intercalate → frameshifts), UV (pyrimidine dimers). Ames test: reversion of his⁻ Salmonella, with liver S9 extract for metabolic activation.
  • Other landmarks: reverse transcriptase (Temin, Baltimore), catalytic RNA (Cech, Altman), prions (Prusiner).

Traps examiners use

  • Klenow lacks the 5′→3′ exonuclease, not proofreading.
  • 5S rRNA is made by Pol III, not Pol I like the other rRNAs.
  • lacOᶜ is cis-dominant; lacI⁻ is recessive in trans.
  • Wobble is at the codon’s 3rd base, which pairs with the anticodon’s 1st (5′) base.
  • Meselson–Stahl after two generations: 50% hybrid, 50% light, zero heavy.

Molecular techniques

Tier 1

Cutting, joining and cloning

  • Type II restriction enzymes cut within palindromic sites; host DNA is protected by methylation (restriction–modification). EcoRI G^AATTC (5′ overhang), PstI CTGCA^G (3′ overhang), SmaI CCC^GGG (blunt). Isoschizomers share a site; neoschizomers share the site but cut differently. A 4-bp site occurs every 4⁴ = 256 bp; a 6-bp site every 4,096 bp.
  • T4 DNA ligase joins ends; alkaline phosphatase removes vector 5′ phosphates to stop self-ligation; Klenow fills in 5′ overhangs; terminal transferase adds tails.
  • pBR322: amp and tet resistance, insertional inactivation. pUC: lacZα blue–white screening with X-gal and IPTG; an insert breaks α-complementation, so recombinant colonies are white.
  • Insert capacity: plasmid up to ~10 kb; λ replacement vector ~23 kb; cosmid ~35–45 kb; BAC ~100–300 kb (F-factor based); YAC ~100 kb to over 1 Mb (needs CEN, ARS, TEL). Ti plasmid of Agrobacterium for plants. Expression in E. coli BL21(DE3) with a T7 promoter, induced by IPTG.
  • Getting DNA in: CaCl₂ competent cells with heat shock, or electroporation. cDNA libraries use reverse transcriptase with oligo(dT) primers and lack introns; genomic libraries include introns and non-coding DNA.

Separating and detecting

  • Agarose gels (0.5–2%) for DNA; polyacrylamide for small DNA and proteins; pulsed-field gels for very large DNA. DNA runs towards the anode (+). Ethidium bromide intercalates; SYBR dyes are safer.
  • SDS-PAGE: SDS denatures and coats proteins with uniform negative charge, so separation is by mass; β-mercaptoethanol or DTT breaks disulfides. 2D gel: isoelectric focusing (by pI) first, then SDS-PAGE. Native PAGE keeps complexes intact.
  • Blots: Southern (DNA; Edwin Southern, 1975), Northern (RNA), Western (protein, antibodies; loading controls β-actin, GAPDH, tubulin), Southwestern (DNA-binding proteins). High stringency = higher temperature and lower salt.
  • Chromatography: gel filtration (largest elute first), ion exchange (DEAE is an anion exchanger that binds negatively charged proteins; CM is a cation exchanger), affinity (His-tag on Ni-NTA eluted with imidazole; GST-tag eluted with glutathione), HPLC.
  • Centrifugation: differential; rate-zonal (sucrose gradient, by S value); isopycnic (CsCl, by buoyant density).

PCR family

  • Steps: denature ~94–95 °C, anneal ~50–65 °C (about 5 °C below primer Tm), extend 72 °C. Copies = start × 2ⁿ at ideal efficiency. Mullis, Nobel 1993.
  • Taq (Thermus aquaticus) lacks proofreading and adds a 3′ A overhang (TA cloning). Pfu and Phusion proofread. Primer Tm by the Wallace rule: 2 × (A+T) + 4 × (G+C) °C for short primers.
  • Troubleshooting: no product → template quality, primer design, Mg²⁺, annealing too high; extra bands → raise annealing temperature, lower Mg²⁺, fewer cycles; primer dimers → redesign or hot-start.
  • RT-PCR uses reverse transcriptase first. qPCR: SYBR Green (binds any dsDNA, needs a melt curve) or TaqMan probes (cleaved by Taq’s 5′→3′ exonuclease). Ct falls as template rises; at 100% efficiency a 1-cycle difference is 2-fold and ~3.3 cycles is 10-fold. Relative quantification by ΔΔCt against a reference gene.

Sequencing and editing

  • Sanger: ddNTPs lack the 3′-OH, so chains stop; now read by capillary electrophoresis with fluorescent ddNTPs. Maxam–Gilbert is chemical cleavage.
  • NGS: Illumina (bridge amplification, reversible-terminator sequencing by synthesis); pyrosequencing (pyrophosphate release → light via luciferase); Ion Torrent (H⁺ release); long reads by PacBio (SMRT) and Oxford Nanopore.
  • CRISPR-Cas9: a ~20 nt guide; SpCas9 needs NGG PAM just 3′ of the target and cuts bluntly ~3 bp upstream of it. NHEJ repair gives indels (knockouts); HDR with a donor gives precise edits. Cas12a uses TTTV and leaves staggered ends. Doudna & Charpentier, Nobel 2020.

Imaging and interaction assays

  • GFP from Aequorea (Shimomura, Chalfie, Tsien; Nobel 2008). Confocal uses a pinhole to reject out-of-focus light (optical sections). FRAP measures mobility; FRET reports proximity below ~10 nm. Super-resolution (STED, PALM/STORM; Nobel 2014).
  • ELISA (direct, indirect, sandwich, competitive); immunoprecipitation and co-IP; ChIP (protein–DNA in vivo); yeast two-hybrid (protein–protein: bait on Gal4 DNA-binding domain, prey on activation domain); flow cytometry and FACS; microarrays and RNA-seq.

Traps examiners use

  • Blue colonies are non-recombinant; white ones carry an insert.
  • In gel filtration the biggest molecules come out first.
  • A higher Ct means less starting template.
  • Extra PCR bands mean annealing is too permissive: raise the temperature.
  • DEAE is an anion exchanger even though DEAE itself is positively charged.

  • Probability shortcut for AaBbCc × AaBbCc: multiply per gene. Fully recessive = (1/4)³ = 1/64; fully heterozygous = (1/2)³ = 1/8. Number of gametes from n heterozygous pairs = 2ⁿ.
  • Polygenic additive traits: n gene pairs give 2n + 1 phenotypic classes in F2; each extreme is (1/4)ⁿ.
  • n alleles of one gene give n(n+1)/2 genotypes (ABO: 3 alleles → 6 genotypes, 4 phenotypes).
  • Penetrance (fraction showing the phenotype), expressivity (degree), pleiotropy (one gene, many traits).
  • Chi-square: df = classes − 1. Critical values at p = 0.05: 3.84 (df 1), 5.99 (df 2), 7.81 (df 3). χ² smaller than critical → fits the ratio.

Linkage and mapping

  • Recombination frequency (RF) = recombinants ÷ total; 1% = 1 cM (map unit). RF never exceeds 50%.
  • Three-point cross: parental classes are most frequent, double crossovers (DCO) least. The middle gene is the one that differs between the DCO and parental classes. Distance A–B includes DCOs in both intervals.
  • Coefficient of coincidence = observed DCO ÷ expected DCO (expected = product of the two RFs); interference = 1 − coincidence.
  • Ordered tetrads (Neurospora): gene–centromere distance = ½ × % second-division segregation asci.
  • Linkage groups equal the haploid chromosome number. Morgan (Drosophila), Sturtevant made the first map.

Pedigrees and sex

  • Autosomal dominant: every generation, affected child has an affected parent. Autosomal recessive: skips generations, consanguinity, unaffected carrier parents.
  • X-linked recessive (haemophilia, colour blindness, DMD): mostly males, passed from carrier mothers, never father to son. X-linked dominant: affected father passes it to all daughters and no sons. Mitochondrial: affected mother to all children; affected father to none. Y-linked: father to all sons.
  • Sex determination: XY with SRY (humans); XX/XO (grasshopper); ZW female (birds, butterflies); X:A ratio via Sxl (Drosophila); haplodiploidy (bees). Barr bodies = number of X − 1.
  • Dosage compensation: mammals inactivate one X; Drosophila males double X expression (MSL complex); C. elegans hermaphrodites halve both X.
  • Aneuploidies: Down (47,+21; or Robertsonian 14/21), Edwards (+18), Patau (+13), Klinefelter (47,XXY; male, one Barr body), Turner (45,X; female, no Barr body). Cri-du-chat: 5p deletion. CML: Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22), BCR–ABL.
  • Inversion heterozygotes: paracentric (centromere outside) crossing over gives a dicentric bridge and acentric fragment; pericentric (includes centromere) gives duplications and deletions.

Bacterial and phage genetics

  • Transformation: uptake of naked DNA by competent cells. Conjugation: F⁺ × F⁻ transfers F only; Hfr transfers chromosome genes in order (interrupted mating maps in minutes; recipient rarely becomes F⁺); F′ carries some chromosomal genes. Transduction: generalised (P1, P22; any host gene) or specialised (λ; genes next to attλ, such as gal and bio). High cotransduction frequency means genes are close.
  • Complementation (cis–trans) test: two recessive mutants in trans give wild type → different genes; no wild type → same gene (cistron). Benzer’s rII mapping in T4 reached single-nucleotide resolution.

Population and quantitative genetics

  • Hardy–Weinberg: p + q = 1; p² + 2pq + q² = 1. Assumes large population, random mating, no mutation, migration or selection. For rare recessive diseases, carrier frequency ≈ 2q.
  • X-linked: male phenotype frequency = q; affected females = q².
  • Genetic drift is strongest in small populations (founder effect, bottleneck). Inbreeding raises homozygosity without changing allele frequencies.
  • Heritability: broad H² = Vg/Vp; narrow h² = Va/Vp (predicts response to selection).

Non-Mendelian inheritance

  • Cytoplasmic: leaf variegation in Mirabilis (Correns), petite yeast, Chlamydomonas chloroplast genes.
  • Maternal effect: offspring phenotype follows the mother’s genotype (snail Limnaea coiling; Drosophila bicoid).
  • Genomic imprinting: Prader–Willi (paternal 15q11–13 lost) and Angelman (maternal lost).

Traps examiners use

  • Maternal effect is not maternal inheritance: the mother’s nuclear genotype decides, one generation late.
  • Klinefelter has a Barr body; Turner does not.
  • In a three-point cross, distance between outer genes must add the DCOs twice (once per interval).
  • Complementation means different genes. Students reverse this.
  • Carrier frequency for a 1 in 10,000 recessive disease is about 1 in 50, not 1 in 100.

Biological chemistry

Tier 1

Biomolecules

  • Essential amino acids: Phe, Val, Thr, Trp, Ile, Met, His, Leu, Lys (Arg semi-essential). Glycine is achiral; proline breaks helices; cysteine forms disulfides.
  • pKa values: α-COOH ~2, α-NH₃⁺ ~9–10; side chains Asp 3.9, Glu 4.1, His 6.0, Cys 8.3, Tyr 10.1, Lys 10.5, Arg 12.5. pI of a neutral amino acid = average of the two pKas around the zwitterion (glycine: (2.34 + 9.60)/2 = 5.97). His is the best buffer near pH 7.
  • Proteins absorb at 280 nm (Trp > Tyr), nucleic acids at 260 nm.
  • α-helix: right-handed, 3.6 residues per turn, 0.15 nm rise per residue, 0.54 nm pitch; C=O of residue i bonds to N–H of i + 4. β-sheets can be parallel or antiparallel. The hydrophobic effect drives tertiary folding. Anfinsen (ribonuclease refolding): sequence determines structure. Chaperones: Hsp70, GroEL/GroES.
  • Haemoglobin binds O₂ cooperatively (sigmoid, Hill coefficient ~2.8); myoglobin is hyperbolic.
  • Sugars: glucose is an aldohexose, fructose a ketohexose. Anomers differ at C1 (α/β); epimers at one other carbon (glucose/galactose at C4, glucose/mannose at C2). Sucrose (Glc α1→β2 Fru) is non-reducing; lactose (Gal β1→4 Glc) and maltose (α1→4) are reducing. Cellulose β1→4; amylose α1→4; amylopectin and glycogen add α1→6 branches (glycogen every 8–12 residues). Chitin: N-acetylglucosamine β1→4.
  • Lipids: essential fatty acids linoleic (ω-6) and α-linolenic (ω-3). Cholesterol gives steroid hormones, bile acids and vitamin D.

Enzymes

  • Enzymes lower activation energy; they do not change ΔG or the equilibrium constant.
  • v = Vmax[S] ÷ (Km + [S]). Km = [S] at half Vmax. At [S] = Km, v = 0.5 Vmax; at [S] = 3Km, v = 0.75 Vmax; at [S] = 9Km, v = 0.9 Vmax.
  • kcat = turnover number; kcat/Km = catalytic efficiency, capped near 10⁸–10⁹ M⁻¹ s⁻¹ by diffusion (triose phosphate isomerase, carbonic anhydrase, catalase, fumarase).
  • Lineweaver–Burk: y-intercept 1/Vmax, x-intercept −1/Km, slope Km/Vmax.

Bioenergetics

  • ΔG = ΔG°′ + RT ln Q; ΔG°′ = −nFΔE°′ (F = 96.5 kJ V⁻¹ mol⁻¹). NADH → O₂ (ΔE°′ ≈ 1.14 V, n = 2) releases ~220 kJ/mol.
  • ATP → ADP: ΔG°′ = −30.5 kJ/mol. Higher-energy donors: PEP (−61.9), 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate (−49.3), creatine phosphate (−43). Glucose-6-phosphate (−13.8) is lower.

Carbohydrate metabolism

  • Glycolysis (cytosol): irreversible steps hexokinase (glucokinase in liver), PFK-1 (committed and rate-limiting; activated by AMP and fructose-2,6-bisphosphate, inhibited by ATP and citrate), pyruvate kinase. Net 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose. Substrate-level phosphorylation by phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase. Arsenate uncouples at GAPDH; iodoacetate inhibits GAPDH; fluoride inhibits enolase.
  • Anaerobic fates: lactate (LDH) or, in yeast, ethanol (pyruvate decarboxylase with TPP, then alcohol dehydrogenase); both regenerate NAD⁺.
  • Pyruvate dehydrogenase complex uses five cofactors: TPP, lipoamide, CoA, FAD, NAD⁺.
  • TCA (matrix): isocitrate DH (rate-limiting) and α-KG DH each give NADH + CO₂; succinyl-CoA synthetase makes GTP; succinate DH (complex II, FAD, membrane-bound, inhibited by malonate); malate DH gives NADH. Per acetyl-CoA: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP, 2 CO₂. Fluorocitrate blocks aconitase. Pyruvate carboxylase refills oxaloacetate (anaplerosis).
  • Gluconeogenesis bypasses: pyruvate carboxylase (biotin; activated by acetyl-CoA) + PEPCK; fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase; glucose-6-phosphatase (liver and kidney, not muscle). Cost: 4 ATP + 2 GTP + 2 NADH per glucose.
  • Pentose phosphate pathway: G6PD (rate-limiting) makes NADPH and ribose-5-phosphate; G6PD deficiency causes haemolysis under oxidative stress.
  • Glycogen: synthase uses UDP-glucose; phosphorylase uses PLP. Glucagon and adrenaline → cAMP → PKA → phosphorylation activates phosphorylase and inactivates synthase.

Oxidative phosphorylation

  • Complex I (NADH–Q; rotenone, amytal; pumps 4 H⁺), II (succinate–Q; no pumping), III (cytochrome bc₁; antimycin A; Q cycle, 4 H⁺), IV (cytochrome c oxidase; cyanide, azide, CO; 2 H⁺ per 2 e⁻), V (ATP synthase; oligomycin blocks Fo). Mitchell’s chemiosmotic theory; Boyer’s binding-change, rotary mechanism.
  • Uncouplers (2,4-DNP, UCP1 thermogenin in brown fat) raise O₂ use but cut ATP synthesis.
  • P/O ≈ 2.5 for NADH and 1.5 for FADH₂; ~30–32 ATP per glucose (older books 36–38). Malate–aspartate shuttle keeps NADH; glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle gives FADH₂.

Lipid and nitrogen metabolism

  • β-oxidation (matrix): carnitine shuttle (CPT-I inhibited by malonyl-CoA). Each round gives 1 FADH₂, 1 NADH, 1 acetyl-CoA. Palmitate (C16): 7 rounds, 8 acetyl-CoA, 106 ATP net (108 − 2 for activation).
  • Fatty acid synthesis (cytosol): acetyl-CoA carboxylase (biotin, rate-limiting, activated by citrate) makes malonyl-CoA; fatty acid synthase uses NADPH and stops at palmitate. Ketone bodies (acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, acetone) made in liver mitochondria. Cholesterol: HMG-CoA reductase is rate-limiting (statins).
  • Transamination needs PLP (ALT, AST). Urea cycle: carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I (activated by N-acetylglutamate) and ornithine transcarbamoylase in mitochondria; argininosuccinate synthetase, lyase and arginase in cytosol. One N from NH₄⁺, the other from aspartate; 3 ATP (4 high-energy bonds) per urea.
  • Purely ketogenic amino acids: leucine and lysine. Phenylketonuria: phenylalanine hydroxylase defect.

Photosynthesis

  • Light reactions (thylakoid): PSII (P680) splits water at the Mn₄CaO₅ cluster, so O₂ comes from water (Ruben & Kamen, ¹⁸O) → plastoquinone → cytochrome b₆f → plastocyanin → PSI (P700) → ferredoxin → FNR → NADPH (Z-scheme). Cyclic flow around PSI makes ATP only, no NADPH or O₂. Emerson’s red drop and enhancement effect showed two photosystems.
  • Calvin cycle (stroma): RuBisCO fixes CO₂ onto RuBP → 2 × 3-phosphoglycerate. Per CO₂: 3 ATP + 2 NADPH; per glucose: 18 ATP + 12 NADPH. RuBisCO’s oxygenase reaction drives photorespiration across chloroplast, peroxisome and mitochondrion.
  • C4 (Hatch–Slack): PEP carboxylase in mesophyll (no O₂ affinity) makes oxaloacetate; malate is decarboxylated in bundle-sheath cells (Kranz anatomy), concentrating CO₂ around RuBisCO; costs 2 extra ATP per CO₂. CAM: stomata open at night, malate stored in vacuoles; temporal separation.

  • Succinate dehydrogenase is both a TCA enzyme and complex II, and it does not pump protons.
  • Muscle lacks glucose-6-phosphatase, so muscle glycogen cannot raise blood glucose.
  • Photosynthetic O₂ comes from water, not CO₂.
  • Leucine and lysine are the only purely ketogenic amino acids.

Developmental biology

Tier 2

Gametes and fertilisation

  • Spermatogenesis: one primary spermatocyte → 4 sperm; Sertoli cells nurse, Leydig cells make testosterone. Oogenesis: oocytes arrest in prophase I (diplotene) until puberty, ovulate at metaphase II, and finish meiosis II only at fertilisation; one ovum plus polar bodies.
  • Sea urchin: acrosome reaction exposes bindin. Mammals: sperm capacitation in the female tract; ZP3 on the zona pellucida triggers the acrosome reaction.
  • Blocks to polyspermy: fast block (membrane depolarisation, sea urchin) and slow block (Ca²⁺ wave → cortical granule exocytosis → fertilisation envelope).

Cleavage and gastrulation

  • Cleavage follows yolk: isolecithal holoblastic (sea urchin; mammals, rotational); mesolecithal unequal holoblastic (amphibians); telolecithal meroblastic discoidal (birds, reptiles, fish); centrolecithal superficial (insects, syncytial blastoderm). Spiral cleavage in molluscs and annelids; radial in echinoderms and amphibians.
  • Mammals: compaction at the 8-cell stage → morula → blastocyst with inner cell mass (Oct4, Nanog; becomes epiblast and hypoblast) and trophectoderm (Cdx2).
  • Gastrulation movements: invagination, involution, ingression, epiboly, delamination.
  • Amphibian axis: sperm entry triggers cortical rotation; the dorsal side forms opposite the entry point (grey crescent). Vegetal dorsal cells with nuclear β-catenin form the Nieuwkoop centre, which induces the Spemann–Mangold organizer in the dorsal lip of the blastopore (1924 newt graft gave a second axis). The organizer secretes BMP antagonists (Noggin, Chordin, Follistatin): ectoderm becomes neural by default when BMP is blocked.
  • Equivalent organizers: Hensen’s node (chick), the node (mouse), the shield (zebrafish).

Vertebrate limb

  • Apical ectodermal ridge (AER; FGF8): proximal–distal outgrowth. Remove it and distal parts are missing; FGF beads rescue.
  • Zone of polarizing activity (ZPA; Shh): anterior–posterior identity. Grafted anteriorly, it gives mirror-image extra digits.
  • Dorsal–ventral: Wnt7a in dorsal ectoderm, Engrailed-1 ventral. Interdigital cells die by apoptosis (BMP). Tbx5 forelimb, Tbx4 and Pitx1 hindlimb.

Other model systems

  • C. elegans: invariant cell lineage (Sulston); 959 somatic cells in the adult hermaphrodite; 131 cells die by programmed cell death (CED-3 = caspase, CED-4 = Apaf-1, CED-9 = Bcl-2; Horvitz). Vulval induction by LIN-3 (EGF-like) from the anchor cell. XX hermaphrodite, XO male.
  • Arabidopsis flower (ABC model): A (APETALA1, APETALA2) → sepals; A + B (APETALA3, PISTILLATA) → petals; B + C → stamens; C (AGAMOUS) → carpels; A and C repress each other; E (SEPALLATA) needed for all. agamous mutant: sepals, petals, petals, then repeats (no stamens or carpels, indeterminate).
  • Stem cells: totipotent (zygote), pluripotent (ICM, ES cells), multipotent (adult stem cells). iPS cells: Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc (Yamanaka); Gurdon’s Xenopus nuclear transfer; Nobel 2012. Dolly (1996) by somatic nuclear transfer.
  • Regeneration: morphallaxis (Hydra), epimorphosis via blastema (salamander limb), compensatory growth (mammalian liver); planarian neoblasts.
  • Metamorphosis: thyroid hormone (T3) in amphibians; ecdysone in insects, with juvenile hormone deciding the type of moult (high JH → larval moult; low → pupal; none → adult). Imaginal discs form adult structures.
  • Mammalian sex: SRY → Sox9 → Sertoli cells → AMH (Müllerian ducts regress) and testosterone (Wolffian ducts develop); DHT (5α-reductase) for external genitalia. Reptiles often use temperature-dependent sex determination (aromatase).
  • Teratogens: thalidomide (limb defects), retinoic acid (alters Hox expression), alcohol (fetal alcohol syndrome).

Animal physiology and immunity

Tier 2

Digestion and respiration

  • Stomach: parietal cells secrete HCl and intrinsic factor (needed for B12 absorption); chief cells secrete pepsinogen. Gastrin stimulates acid. Secretin → pancreatic bicarbonate; CCK → pancreatic enzymes and gall-bladder contraction. Enteropeptidase activates trypsinogen in the duodenum. Fats leave enterocytes as chylomicrons into lacteals.
  • Lung volumes: tidal ~500 mL; vital capacity = inspiratory reserve + tidal + expiratory reserve; residual volume cannot be measured by spirometry. Surfactant (dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine from type II alveolar cells) lowers surface tension.
  • O₂ transport: ~98% bound to haemoglobin; P50 ~26–27 mmHg. Right shift (lower affinity, more unloading): more CO₂, more H⁺ (lower pH), higher temperature, more 2,3-BPG (Bohr effect). Fetal Hb and myoglobin are left-shifted; fetal Hb binds 2,3-BPG poorly. CO binds Hb ~200–250× more strongly than O₂.
  • CO₂ transport: ~70% as bicarbonate (carbonic anhydrase in red cells, chloride shift), ~20% carbamino-Hb, ~7–10% dissolved. Haldane effect: deoxygenated Hb carries more CO₂.
  • Control: medullary centres; central chemoreceptors sense CSF H⁺ (from CO₂); peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid and aortic bodies) sense low PO₂.

Circulation and blood

  • Conduction: SA node → AV node (delay) → bundle of His → Purkinje fibres. ECG: P = atrial depolarisation; QRS = ventricular depolarisation; T = ventricular repolarisation.
  • Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume (~5 L/min). Frank–Starling: more filling → stronger contraction. First heart sound = AV valves close; second = semilunar valves close.
  • Clotting ends with thrombin turning fibrinogen into fibrin; Ca²⁺ is required. Haemophilia A lacks factor VIII, B lacks factor IX. Red cells live ~120 days; erythropoietin comes from the kidney.

Kidney and excretion

  • GFR ~125 mL/min (~180 L/day). Inulin clearance measures GFR; PAH clearance measures renal plasma flow.
  • Proximal tubule reabsorbs ~65% of Na⁺ and water and normally all glucose and amino acids. Loop of Henle is the countercurrent multiplier: descending limb permeable to water, thick ascending limb impermeable to water and pumping NaCl via NKCC2 (furosemide target).
  • ADH (vasopressin) inserts aquaporin-2 in the collecting duct. Aldosterone (zona glomerulosa) boosts Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ secretion. RAAS: renin (juxtaglomerular cells) → angiotensin I → ACE (lungs) → angiotensin II. ANP opposes it.
  • Nitrogen waste: ammonotelic (most fish), ureotelic (mammals, adult amphibians), uricotelic (birds, reptiles, insects).

Nerve and muscle

  • Resting potential ~−70 mV (K⁺ leak plus Na⁺/K⁺ pump). Action potential: voltage-gated Na⁺ in (blocked by tetrodotoxin), then voltage-gated K⁺ out (blocked by TEA). Absolute refractory period comes from Na⁺ channel inactivation. Saltatory conduction at nodes of Ranvier. Hodgkin and Huxley: squid giant axon.
  • Synapse: Ca²⁺ entry triggers SNARE-mediated release. Botulinum and tetanus toxins cleave SNAREs (botulinum → flaccid paralysis; tetanus blocks inhibitory interneurons → spastic paralysis).
  • Transmitters: ACh (nicotinic = ligand-gated cation channel, blocked by curare; muscarinic = GPCR; acetylcholinesterase blocked by organophosphates); GABA (main inhibitory in brain; GABA-A is a Cl⁻ channel); glycine (inhibitory in spinal cord; strychnine blocks); glutamate (main excitatory; NMDA receptors need Mg²⁺ removed and let Ca²⁺ in, central to LTP).
  • Sarcomere: A band (myosin length) stays constant; I band and H zone shorten. Sliding filament theory (Huxley & Hanson; Huxley & Niedergerke). Excitation–contraction: T-tubule DHP receptor → ryanodine receptor on SR → Ca²⁺ binds troponin C → tropomyosin moves. ATP is needed for cross-bridge release (rigor mortis without it). Smooth muscle uses calmodulin and myosin light-chain kinase.

Endocrinology

  • Hypothalamus → anterior pituitary: TRH → TSH, CRH → ACTH, GnRH → FSH and LH, GHRH → GH (somatostatin inhibits GH; dopamine inhibits prolactin). Posterior pituitary releases oxytocin and ADH made in hypothalamic neurons.
  • Adrenal cortex: glomerulosa → aldosterone, fasciculata → cortisol, reticularis → androgens. Medulla (neural crest) → adrenaline and noradrenaline. Thyroid: T3/T4 and calcitonin (lowers Ca²⁺); parathyroid hormone raises Ca²⁺. Pancreas: α → glucagon, β → insulin (receptor tyrosine kinase; moves GLUT4 to the membrane in muscle and fat), δ → somatostatin.
  • Receptor location: peptides and catecholamines bind surface receptors; steroids and thyroid hormones bind intracellular receptors. cAMP: glucagon, β-adrenergic, ADH (V2), TSH, ACTH. IP₃/Ca²⁺: α1-adrenergic, ADH (V1), oxytocin. cGMP: ANP.

Immunity

  • Innate: barriers, neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells, complement, interferons α/β. Toll-like receptors: TLR4 (LPS), TLR3 (dsRNA), TLR5 (flagellin), TLR9 (unmethylated CpG DNA).
  • MHC I on all nucleated cells presents cytosolic peptides (proteasome → TAP) to CD8⁺ T cells. MHC II on dendritic cells, macrophages and B cells presents endocytosed antigen to CD4⁺ T cells. Thymus: positive selection in the cortex, negative selection in the medulla.
  • Antibodies: two heavy and two light chains; Fab binds antigen, Fc drives effector functions. IgG (most abundant, crosses placenta), IgA (secretions and colostrum, dimer with J chain), IgM (pentamer, first in primary response, best at complement), IgE (allergy, parasites, mast cells), IgD (naive B-cell receptor).
  • Diversity: V(D)J recombination by RAG1/2 (Tonegawa, Nobel 1987) plus junctional diversity (TdT); somatic hypermutation and class switching by AID in germinal centres. Burnet’s clonal selection.
  • T helper subsets: Th1 (IFN-γ; intracellular pathogens), Th2 (IL-4, IL-5, IL-13; IgE, parasites), Th17 (IL-17; extracellular bacteria, fungi, autoimmunity), Treg (FoxP3, IL-10, TGF-β).
  • Complement: classical (C1q on antibody), lectin (MBL), alternative (spontaneous C3) → C3 convertase → membrane attack complex C5b–C9.
  • Hypersensitivity: type I IgE (anaphylaxis, hay fever); II antibody against cell-surface antigens (transfusion reaction, haemolytic disease of the newborn); III immune complexes (serum sickness, SLE); IV T-cell delayed (tuberculin test, contact dermatitis).
  • Monoclonal antibodies: hybridomas (Köhler & Milstein, Nobel 1984); HAT medium kills unfused myeloma cells because they lack HGPRT.

Traps examiners use

  • A band stays constant during contraction; only I band and H zone shorten.
  • Right shift means lower O₂ affinity, not higher.
  • Most CO₂ travels as bicarbonate in plasma, not bound to haemoglobin.
  • Thyroid hormone is an amino acid derivative but acts on a nuclear receptor.
  • Tuberculin test is type IV (T cells), not antibody-mediated.

Microbiology

Tier 2

History and classification

  • Leeuwenhoek (first observations); Pasteur (swan-neck flask against spontaneous generation; pasteurisation; anthrax and rabies vaccines); Koch (postulates; anthrax, TB; pure culture on solid media, agar suggested by Fanny Hesse); Lister (antisepsis); Fleming (penicillin, 1928); Ivanovsky and Beijerinck (filterable agent of tobacco mosaic); Winogradsky (soil microbes, enrichment culture).
  • Woese’s three domains (16S/18S rRNA): Bacteria, Archaea (ether-linked isoprenoid lipids, no peptidoglycan, histone-like proteins), Eukarya.

Bacterial cell

  • Peptidoglycan: NAM–NAG chains (β1→4) cross-linked by peptides. Lysozyme cuts the β1→4 bond; penicillin blocks transpeptidation; vancomycin binds D-Ala-D-Ala.
  • Gram-positive: thick peptidoglycan with teichoic acids. Gram-negative: thin peptidoglycan, periplasm, outer membrane with LPS (lipid A = endotoxin, core, O-antigen) and porins.
  • Gram stain: crystal violet → iodine (mordant) → alcohol (decolourise) → safranin. Positive = purple, negative = pink. Acid-fast stain (Ziehl–Neelsen) for mycolic acid–rich Mycobacterium. Mycoplasma has no wall (sterols in its membrane) and resists β-lactams.
  • Endospores (Bacillus, Clostridium): dehydrated core with calcium dipicolinate; survive boiling. Flagella spin using proton motive force; sex pili for conjugation; capsules aid virulence (Griffith’s smooth strain).

Growth and control

  • Growth curve: lag, log (exponential), stationary, death. Generations n = (log N − log N₀) ÷ 0.301 = 3.3 × log(N/N₀); generation time = elapsed time ÷ n.
  • Oxygen classes: obligate aerobes, facultative anaerobes (E. coli), microaerophiles, aerotolerant anaerobes, obligate anaerobes (lack catalase and superoxide dismutase). Temperature: psychrophiles, mesophiles, thermophiles, hyperthermophiles.
  • Media: selective and differential MacConkey (bile salts and crystal violet select Gram-negatives; lactose fermenters pink); EMB (E. coli metallic green sheen); blood agar haemolysis (α partial, green; β complete, clear; γ none).
  • Sterilisation: autoclave 121 °C, 15 psi, 15–20 min (kills spores; indicator Geobacillus stearothermophilus); hot-air oven 160–170 °C for ~2 h; 0.22 µm filtration for heat-labile liquids (does not remove viruses); ethylene oxide gas; gamma rays (penetrating); UV (surfaces, thymine dimers). Pasteurisation (63 °C for 30 min, or 72 °C for 15 s) reduces but does not sterilise. D-value = time to kill 90%.

Viruses and other agents

  • Baltimore classes: I dsDNA (herpes, pox), II ssDNA (parvovirus), III dsRNA (rotavirus), IV +ssRNA (polio, SARS-CoV-2), V −ssRNA (influenza, rabies; must carry their own RNA-dependent RNA polymerase), VI +ssRNA with reverse transcriptase (HIV), VII dsDNA with reverse transcriptase (hepatitis B).
  • Phage: lytic (T4) or temperate (λ, lysogeny with a prophage). One-step growth curve: eclipse, latent period, burst size. Plaque assay counts PFU.
  • HIV gp120 binds CD4 with CCR5 or CXCR4 co-receptors; reverse transcriptase, integrase and protease are drug targets. Influenza: 8 segments; antigenic drift = point mutations; antigenic shift = segment reassortment (pandemics). SARS-CoV-2 spike binds ACE2.
  • Viroids: small circular RNA, no protein coat (potato spindle tuber). Prions: misfolded PrPSc protein, no nucleic acid.

Disease and antibiotics

  • Exotoxins (proteins, often A–B, heat-labile, can be made into toxoids): cholera (Gs locked on → cAMP → Cl⁻ and water loss), diphtheria (ADP-ribosylates eEF2), tetanus and botulinum (see physiology). Endotoxin: lipid A of Gram-negative LPS, heat-stable, causes fever and shock.
  • Diseases worth knowing: TB (M. tuberculosis; BCG), typhoid (Salmonella Typhi), cholera (Vibrio cholerae), plague (Yersinia pestis), leprosy (M. leprae), syphilis (Treponema pallidum), malaria (Plasmodium via female Anopheles; P. falciparum most severe; Ronald Ross), kala-azar (Leishmania donovani, sandfly), sleeping sickness (Trypanosoma brucei, tsetse fly), Chagas disease (T. cruzi), amoebic dysentery (Entamoeba histolytica).
  • Antibiotic targets: wall (β-lactams, vancomycin, bacitracin); 30S (aminoglycosides, tetracyclines); 50S (macrolides, chloramphenicol, linezolid); DNA gyrase and topo IV (quinolones); RNA polymerase (rifampicin); folate synthesis (sulfonamides block dihydropteroate synthase as PABA analogues; trimethoprim blocks DHFR); mycolic acid (isoniazid); membrane (polymyxin). Resistance: β-lactamases, efflux pumps, altered targets (MRSA’s PBP2a), spread by plasmids and transposons.

Environmental and applied

  • Nitrogen fixation by nitrogenase (O₂-sensitive, needs Mo and Fe): Rhizobium in legume nodules (leghaemoglobin buffers O₂), free-living Azotobacter and Clostridium, cyanobacterial heterocysts. Nitrification: Nitrosomonas (NH₃ → NO₂⁻), Nitrobacter (NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻). Denitrification (Pseudomonas, Paracoccus): NO₃⁻ → N₂. Anammox oxidises NH₄⁺ with NO₂⁻.
  • Methanogens are archaea. Industrial microbes: Saccharomyces (ethanol), Lactobacillus (lactic acid, curd), Aspergillus niger (citric acid), Penicillium chrysogenum (penicillin), Streptomyces griseus (streptomycin), Bacillus thuringiensis (Cry insecticidal proteins). Chakrabarty’s engineered Pseudomonas for oil degradation. BOD measures organic pollution; coliform tests indicate faecal contamination.

  • 0.22 µm filters remove bacteria but not viruses.

  • Nitrosomonas makes nitrite from ammonia; Nitrobacter makes nitrate from nitrite. Students swap them.
  • Antigenic shift (reassortment) causes pandemics; drift causes seasonal change.

Ecology

Tier 3

Abiotic factors and rules

  • Liebig’s law of the minimum (the scarcest resource limits growth); Shelford’s law of tolerance (both too little and too much limit). Eury- = wide tolerance, steno- = narrow.
  • Bergmann’s rule: larger bodies in colder climates. Allen’s rule: shorter extremities in colder climates. Gloger’s rule: darker pigmentation in warm, humid regions.

Ecosystems and energy

  • NPP = GPP − autotroph respiration. Highest NPP per area: tropical rainforests, estuaries, coral reefs, swamps and marshes. The open ocean is low per area but large in total; deserts and tundra are lowest.
  • Energy flows one way; about 10% passes to the next trophic level (Lindeman, 1942). Pyramids: numbers can be inverted (one tree, many insects); biomass can be inverted in open water (phytoplankton turn over fast); energy is never inverted.
  • Cycles: carbon and nitrogen have large atmospheric pools; phosphorus is sedimentary with no significant gas phase. Biomagnification concentrates DDT and mercury up food chains. Eutrophication: nutrient enrichment → algal blooms → low O₂.

Populations

  • Exponential: dN/dt = rN; Nₜ = N₀e^(rt); doubling time = ln 2 ÷ r ≈ 0.693 ÷ r. Logistic: dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K); growth is fastest at N = K/2.
  • r-selected species: many small offspring, little care, unstable habitats. K-selected: few large offspring, much care, stable habitats.
  • Survivorship curves: type I (most survive to old age; humans), type II (constant risk; many birds, Hydra), type III (huge early mortality; oysters, most fish, many plants).
  • Lincoln–Petersen estimate: N = (marked in first sample × total in second) ÷ marked recaptured.

Communities

  • Interactions: mutualism (+/+), commensalism (+/0), amensalism (−/0), competition (−/−), predation and parasitism (+/−).
  • Gause’s competitive exclusion principle (Paramecium aurelia beats P. caudatum). Hutchinson’s fundamental versus realised niche. Resource partitioning (MacArthur’s warblers). Keystone species: Paine’s removal of the sea star Pisaster.
  • Succession: primary (bare rock; lichens as pioneers) is slower than secondary (abandoned field). Hydrarch starts in water, xerarch on dry rock; both end at a climax community.
  • Diversity: alpha (within a site), beta (turnover between sites), gamma (regional); Shannon and Simpson indices. Island biogeography (MacArthur & Wilson): species richness rises with island size and falls with distance; species–area relation S = cA^z.

Regional ecology

  • Biomes: tropical rainforest, savanna, desert, temperate grassland, temperate deciduous forest, taiga (boreal conifer), tundra (permafrost).
  • Lakes: littoral, limnetic, profundal and benthic zones. Summer stratification: epilimnion, metalimnion (thermocline), hypolimnion; spring and autumn turnover. Streams are lotic; lakes and ponds lentic. Marine: intertidal, neritic, oceanic; the photic zone supports photosynthesis.
  • India’s biodiversity hotspots: Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Sundaland (Nicobar Islands). Conservation in situ (national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves) and ex situ (seed banks, zoos).

Plant physiology

Tier 3

Water relations and transport

  • Water potential ψ = ψs + ψp (plus gravity for tall trees). Pure water = 0 MPa; solutes make ψs negative; water moves from higher to lower ψ. ψs = −iCRT.
  • Routes: apoplast, symplast, transmembrane. The Casparian strip (suberin) in the endodermis forces water and ions through the symplast.
  • Ascent of sap: cohesion–tension theory (Dixon & Joly) driven by transpiration pull. Root pressure explains guttation through hydathodes; capillarity contributes little.
  • Stomata: blue light activates the guard-cell H⁺-ATPase → K⁺ (and Cl⁻, malate) enter → water follows → guard cells swell → pore opens. ABA closes them (Ca²⁺ signalling, anion and K⁺ efflux).
  • Phloem: Münch pressure-flow hypothesis, source to sink; sucrose is loaded actively by H⁺–sucrose symport into sieve tubes with companion cells. Aphid stylets sample phloem sap.

Mineral nutrition

  • Macronutrients: C, H, O, N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S. Micronutrients: Fe, Mn, Cu, Zn, B, Mo, Cl, Ni.
  • Mobile elements (N, P, K, Mg) show deficiency first in older leaves; immobile ones (Ca, S, Fe, B) in young leaves.
  • Roles: Mg in chlorophyll; Mo in nitrogenase and nitrate reductase; Mn in PSII’s water-splitting cluster; Fe in cytochromes and ferredoxin; Cu in plastocyanin; Ca in middle-lamella calcium pectate; Zn for auxin synthesis; Ni in urease; B for pollen tubes and Ca use.
  • Deficiency signs: N → older-leaf chlorosis; Fe → interveinal chlorosis of young leaves; Zn → little leaf; B → heart rot of beet; Mn → grey speck of oats; Mo → whiptail of cauliflower.
  • Nitrogen assimilation: nitrate reductase (cytosol) → nitrite reductase (plastid) → NH₄⁺ → GS–GOGAT (glutamine synthetase, glutamate synthase).

Flowering and movement

  • Photoperiodism (Garner & Allard, Maryland Mammoth tobacco). Short-day plants (really long-night plants): Xanthium, chrysanthemum, soybean. Long-day: spinach, wheat, Hyoscyamus. Day-neutral: tomato, cucumber. Leaves perceive the photoperiod.
  • Phytochrome: Pr absorbs red (~660 nm) → Pfr (active), which absorbs far-red (~730 nm) → Pr. A red-light night break prevents flowering in short-day plants; far-red after it reverses the effect (the last flash counts).
  • Florigen is the FT protein, moving from leaf to shoot apex in phloem. Vernalisation (prolonged cold) silences the floral repressor FLC in Arabidopsis.
  • Tropisms: phototropism (blue-light phototropins), gravitropism (amyloplast statoliths in root cap), thigmotropism. Nastic movements: Mimosa leaf folding (turgor loss in the pulvinus), sleep movements.
  • Respiratory quotient (CO₂ out ÷ O₂ in): carbohydrates 1, fats ~0.7, proteins ~0.8–0.9, organic acids above 1 (malic acid 1.33).

Traps examiners use

  • Short-day plants respond to night length; interrupting the night with red light stops flowering.
  • Iron deficiency appears in young leaves; nitrogen deficiency in old leaves.
  • High auxin:cytokinin gives roots, not shoots.

Biophysics

Tier 3

Forces and conformation

  • Approximate energies: covalent C–C ~350 kJ/mol; hydrogen bond ~4–20; van der Waals ~0.4–4. The hydrophobic effect is mainly entropy-driven (ordered water is released).
  • The peptide bond is planar with partial double-bond character, almost always trans. Ramachandran plot: φ (N–Cα) and ψ (Cα–C); α-helix near φ −57°, ψ −47°; β-sheet near φ −120°, ψ +120°. Glycine is most flexible, proline most restricted.
  • Folding: Levinthal’s paradox; molten globule intermediates; net folding free energy is small (~20–60 kJ/mol). Urea and guanidinium chloride denature.
  • DNA melting: absorbance at 260 nm rises (hyperchromic effect) on denaturation. Tm rises with GC content and salt and falls with formamide. Repetitive DNA renatures fastest (C₀t curves).
  • Membranes: transition temperature rises with longer, saturated chains; cholesterol broadens the transition.

Microscopy, isotopes and sedimentation

  • Resolution d = 0.61λ/NA (Abbe: λ/2NA); light microscopes reach ~200 nm. Phase contrast (Zernike) shows unstained living cells; TEM for thin sections, SEM for surfaces.
  • Isotopes: ³H (12.3 years), ¹⁴C (5,730 years), ³²P (14.3 days), ³⁵S (87 days), ¹²⁵I (60 days, γ). Remaining fraction = (½)^(t/T).
  • Sedimentation coefficient S = v/ω²r (1 S = 10⁻¹³ s); S depends on shape and mass, so it does not add (30S + 50S = 70S).
  • Protein structure prediction: AlphaFold (Nobel 2024 with protein design by Baker).

Traps examiners use

  • Denatured DNA absorbs more UV, not less.
  • CD gives secondary structure content, not an atomic structure.
  • NMR works in solution; X-ray needs crystals.

Biostatistics and experimental design

Bonus

Not a named syllabus subject, but questions on controls and statistics appear in many PhD tests and in nearly every interview.

  • Mean, median, mode; median resists outliers and suits skewed data. Standard deviation measures spread; standard error of the mean = SD ÷ √n measures precision of the mean.
  • Normal distribution: ~68% within ±1 SD, ~95% within ±1.96 SD, ~99.7% within ±3 SD.
  • Tests: t-test (two means; paired if the same samples are measured twice), ANOVA (three or more means), chi-square (counts versus expected), correlation (strength of association, r from −1 to +1; r² = variance explained), regression (predicting one variable from another).
  • p-value: probability of data at least this extreme if the null hypothesis is true. Type I error: rejecting a true null (false positive, rate α). Type II error: missing a real effect (false negative, rate β); power = 1 − β, raised by larger samples.
  • Controls: a positive control shows the assay can detect an effect; a negative control shows what “no effect” looks like; vehicle controls test the solvent. Biological replicates (independent samples) matter more than technical replicates (repeat measurements).
